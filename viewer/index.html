How to read the measures

Boxes only draws the recorded rectangles in grey. Hovering or clicking a box names its word and line. Every other option colours each box by a measure computed on the text of the current bundle; the controls that a measure needs appear under Configuration.

MATTR is the number of distinct spellings in a window of the chosen size, divided by its word count. Backward includes the clicked word and the preceding words; forward includes it and the following words; centered splits the remaining words around it. The window follows the text's order (Path 1 for the manuscript bundle) continuously across page and bifolium boundaries. Repeated spellings marks whether each word appears more than once in its own window; the overlay keeps MATTR fills and outlines repeated spellings in red. The mean beside each row averages only complete windows contained within that row's pages.

Word repetition impact replaces one occurrence with a spelling absent from the whole text and reports the fraction of its containing windows whose score would rise. Step change is the current backward-window score minus the preceding one: the new type the arriving word adds against the type lost as a word leaves.

Spatial sources (Direction) replace the reading-order window with the words in view of the target on the same page: earlier words to its left within the left reach, and words above it whose recorded rectangle intersects a cone of the chosen depth and half-angle. Weights fall with distance, 1/(1+d)² to the left and cos²(angle)/(1+d)² above, normalised by the page's median word width or line spacing; equal weights are the control. Minimum source edit distance and copy/edit source candidates compare the target with the words in its cone by glyph-unit edits.

Component diversity is 2 raised to the Shannon entropy of within-word glyph pairs among the words in view or in the window; Glyph H1 is single-unit entropy and Glyph H2 the conditional next-unit entropy, in bits; Component JS distance is the Jensen–Shannon distance between the local pair distribution and the whole text's. Units follow the lossless Zattera parser for Voynich text and letters for natural-language controls. Fewer than five contributing words or fewer than three effective sources are left unscored. These colours use a linear scale from the 5th to the 95th percentile of valid scores across the whole text.

Order. Manuscript bifolia (the default) is the manuscript's sheets by their first folio, each sheet's pages kept together; Manuscript pages lists single pages in the page sequence, so a sheet's pages lie apart; Path 1 is the positional model's working order of the 50 bifolia (Golf 2.1, not an established chronology), the order the 2026 studies used. The token stream of a bundle, and so every window, keeps the order it was built in (the default bundle is by bifolium). A sheet's row mean gathers its words wherever they fall.

Coordinates. The default bundle holds every page the Voynichese Project recorded plus the Rosettes, with every locus that has a fully read word. Recorded draws its boxes as on the photographs; Flat draws them with each page's photographic warp removed by the toolkit's page warp product. The spatial measures (sources, cones, pools) are computed in the flat state either way. A page without a fitted warp field stays put, and the status line says so. The page image is a photograph, so flat coordinates cannot sit on it: with Page image ticked the boxes and overlays return to recorded coordinates, and unticking it returns to Flat. A text that carries one set of coordinates has no choice to offer.

Lines, baselines and warp. These Colors read how each line lies on the page, on the most complete reviewed geometry with a shared box counted once. Each horizontal line has baseline anchors: the ink baseline measured on the Beinecke scans where a page has clean ink records, the glyph-corrected box baseline elsewhere, never the box bottom. Word colours show the distance from the line's chord or fit, the shift the warp removed and the ink against the box. Line colours show the chord in both states, the fit, the end offset, the shape of the line and its breaks, the ink angle and the x-height spread. Page colours show the stored warp. H is the page's median box height. An angle is positive when the line falls to the right, a residual when the word lies below the line, sag when the middle lies lower on the page than the ends, a step when the text after a break sits lower. Row means weight every line once. Overlays draw the chords, fits, anchors, warped baselines, breaks, component fits, intrusions and ink fits in the state shown. Clicking a word opens its line's numbers, its paragraph position and what its page shares. The same numbers come from vmsspatial.line_stats.

Text. Control bundles are texts typeset into the manuscript's line frames by the toolkit: generator streams, word-bigram Markov chains filled to each line's character budget, and natural prose either filled to the budget or kept in its own printed lines. Each bundle brings its own geometry and is scored on its own.

From this text. A bundle may carry its own measures (one value per word) and overlays (lines and markers in page coordinates). The measures join the Colors menu under this heading, with row means and a legend from their range; the overlays draw on an expanded page (Page overlay → Overlays, one or several) and follow the Voynichese image when it is shown; clicking a word shows the line's notes when the bundle has them. The toolkit writes such a bundle with viewer_data.study_data, and #data=<url> in this page's address opens another data folder; expanded=<folio>&line=<locus> opens a page on one of its lines.

Word direction. Words on rings and rotated lines are sized along their own direction, not by the width of their box, so the words at 3 and 9 o'clock are as large as those at 12 o'clock. Bottom to centre turns each word the way it is written; Level stands the same words upright to read them. Only the labels turn: the boxes stay where they were measured.

Zoom and loci. On an expanded page the mouse wheel zooms about the cursor and dragging pans; Reset view returns to the whole page. The Locus menu lists the page's lines with their ZL locus type and orientation (horizontal, rotated, circular, radial, label; the text angle in degrees, positive = falling to the right) and frames the boxes of one locus, so a ring of a circular page or a label group of the Rosettes can be inspected box by box; the overlays and the page image follow. The address keeps the view: zoom=<locus> frames a locus, view=x,y,w,h restores a viewBox. Clicking a word shows its line's orientation record (kind, angle, confidence and the cues that decided) beside the line notes; the box tooltip carries the kind and angle. On the Rosettes the thin amber boxes are detected positions (not recorded coordinates) and the status line gives the detection coverage.
