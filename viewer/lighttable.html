How to read this

Sheets. Each sheet is one bifolium opened flat: two leaves of the catalogue's 160 × 225 mm, the fold dashed between them. The face that is up is drawn as read, its words filled. The face underneath is mirrored about the fold, its words in outline, so each of its pages lies behind its partner on the same leaf, as if the sheet were held up to the light: the outer face is Bv | Ar and the inner face Av | Br, so Av lies behind Ar and Br behind Bv. Turn a sheet over to bring the other face up.

Placement. Every page is in millimetres from its fold and its leaf's top edge, measured on the Beinecke scans or estimated, as on the Page stream's parchment scale. The strip of a leaf hidden in the binding is not measured, so a gap across a fold can be a little short. Foldout panels lie outward from the fold, in panel order; every foldout leaf is drawn at its widths measured on the Beinecke photographs, with its creases and further folds dashed, each page on its own panels; f101r and f101v each run across both panels of their leaf. On f101 the binding hides about 13 mm more of the panel by the binding on the recto than on the verso, so that panel is drawn at each face's own width. The Rosettes sheet (N:85-86) is drawn folded as it is. That sheet is drawn as the Beinecke photographs show it, three panels across and two down. The Rosettes fill its inside (f85v | f86r), drawn whole as photographed, so that they run on across the creases. Outside, the lower row (f86v5, f86v3, f85r1) reads the same way up as the Rosettes, and the upper row (f86v6, f86v4, f85r2) is written upside down, to be read with the upper half folded down over the lower. Flat coordinates take each page's shared curve and tilt out of the vertical positions; Recorded keeps them as drawn.

Leaves and copies. Divide cuts a sheet at its fold into its two leaves (folios), each left where it lay; Join makes it whole again. A leaf turns over where it lies, so its bound edge goes to the other side. The arrow beside Add sheet offers its two leaves, to put one on the table on its own. The same bifolium or leaf can go on the table more than once, each copy in its own colour; the two leaves of one sheet share its colour.

Moving and zooming. Drag a sheet or leaf, or select it and use the arrow keys: 0.5 mm a step, 5 mm with Shift, 0.1 mm with Alt. F turns it over, D divides or joins, Delete removes it; a click on the empty table lets go of it. A piece goes exactly where it is put. With Guides on, a line shows where one of its folds or edges comes within about a millimetre of another piece's, or its top edge of the other's top or bottom edge, with how far off it is; the line turns solid and reads aligned when they meet. With Snap to guides on (Settings; off unless chosen), a piece being dragged jumps onto such a line. The wheel zooms about the pointer; drag the empty table, or drag anywhere with the middle button, to pan. Each row gives the piece's fold edge and top edge in millimetres from the first piece's. The arrangement stays in the address, so a link reopens it. Positions are display choices: nothing here is a statistical test.

Pages shown. Outer face and Inner face show or hide the text of every sheet's outer face (Bv | Ar) or inner face (Av | Br). In each row, Outer and Inner do the same for one sheet, and each page has its own box.

Colours and words. One per sheet gives each piece its own colour; click a piece's colour square to choose another, one of the six sheet colours or ink, or any colour with Custom. Outer and inner faces colours every page by the face of its sheet: Bv and Ar on the outer face one colour, Av and Br on the inner face another, wherever the piece lies and whichever side is up; click a square beside the menu to choose either face's colour. Where pieces overlap, the filled colour shows which side of each sheet is up, and the outlines behind show the other; the outlines of the leaves are then in ink. Words writes each word's EVA spelling in its box once the zoom makes it big enough to read, from about 2 px/mm. Words on the face underneath are written the right way round, for reading, at their mirrored places. On rings and turned lines a word is written along the writing, its bottom towards the wheel's centre, at one size for the page, sized by its length along that direction rather than its box's width; Word direction: Level stands those words upright at the same size, where they can run past their boxes.
