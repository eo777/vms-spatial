Using the table

Selecting and moving. Click a piece to select it, and Shift-click to add or take away another. Drag on the empty table to draw a box: every piece it touches is selected. Drag a selected piece to move them all. The arrow keys move the selection 0.5 mm a step, 5 mm with Shift, 0.1 mm with Alt. A click on the empty table, or Escape, lets go. A piece goes exactly where it is put. With Guides on (View), a line shows where one of its folds or edges comes within about a millimetre of another piece's, or its top edge of the other's top or bottom edge, with how far off it is; the line turns solid and reads aligned when they meet, and goes when you let go. With Snap to guides on (Edit), a piece being dragged jumps onto such a line.

Panning and zooming. Drag with the right or middle button, or hold Space and drag, to pan. The wheel zooms about the pointer; − and + zoom too, and Fit (Shift 1) shows the selection, or every piece. The table has no edge.

The selection's toolbar. Over the selected pieces, a toolbar sets their colour (one of the six sheet colours, ink, or any colour with Custom) and opacity, turns them over (F), shows both faces, divides a sheet into its leaves or joins them (D), chooses which of a piece's pages are drawn, duplicates them (Ctrl D) and removes them (Delete). Both faces puts each piece's other face beside it, to the right, linked: the two move, turn and change together, and nothing is drawn behind either, so each physical face can be read on its own at once. Duplicate puts a copy of each, as it is, to the right of the selection, and selects the copies. Edit has the same, with Select all (Ctrl A), Stack at the fold and Spread out, which act on the selection when two or more are selected and on every piece otherwise.

Adding. Add sheet opens the list of bifolia, with a search box: type a name (A:1-8), a folio (f68, or 68) or a section. Click a bifolium's name to add the whole sheet, or one of its folios to add that leaf alone. The same bifolium or leaf can go on the table more than once, each copy in its own colour; the two leaves of one sheet share it. Pieces lists them, with the faces and pages each shows, where each lies against the first, and the same controls; a name there selects the piece and brings it into view.

Sheets. Each sheet is one bifolium opened flat: two leaves of the catalogue's 160 × 225 mm, the fold dashed between them. The face that is up is drawn as read, its words filled. The face underneath is mirrored about the fold, its words in outline, so each of its pages lies behind its partner on the same leaf, as if the sheet were held up to the light: the outer face is Bv | Ar and the inner face Av | Br, so Av lies behind Ar and Br behind Bv. A sheet turns over about its fold. Divide cuts it at the fold into its two leaves, each left where it lay, and a leaf turns over where it lies, so its bound edge goes to the other side; Join makes the sheet whole again.

Placement. Every page is in millimetres from its fold and its leaf's top edge, measured on the Beinecke scans or estimated, as on the Page stream's parchment scale. The strip of a leaf hidden in the binding is not measured, so a gap across a fold can be a little short. Foldout panels lie outward from the fold, in panel order; every foldout leaf is drawn at its widths measured on the Beinecke photographs, with its creases and further folds dashed, each page on its own panels; f101r and f101v each run across both panels of their leaf. On f101 the binding hides about 13 mm more of the panel by the binding on the recto than on the verso, so that panel is drawn at each face's own width. The Rosettes sheet (N:85-86) is drawn folded as it is, three panels across and two down, as the Beinecke photographs show it. The Rosettes fill its inside (f85v | f86r), drawn whole as photographed, so that they run on across the creases. Outside, the lower row (f86v5, f86v3, f85r1) reads the same way up as the Rosettes, and the upper row (f86v6, f86v4, f85r2) is written upside down, to be read with the upper half folded down over the lower. Flat coordinates take each page's shared curve and tilt out of the vertical positions; Recorded keeps them as drawn.

What is drawn (View). Outer face and Inner face show or hide the text of every sheet's outer face (Bv | Ar) or inner face (Av | Br); Visible face and Face underneath, the text of the face up on each piece and of the face seen through it, whichever physical face that is. The Opacity sliders set the strength of the word boxes on either face, the margins and angles, and the leaves' tint for every piece at once, and Dim word boxes steps every box back so the margins and angles stand out. One per sheet gives each piece its own colour; Outer and inner faces colours every page by the face of its sheet, Bv and Ar one colour and Av and Br another, wherever the piece lies and whichever side is up, so where pieces overlap the filled colour shows which side of each sheet is up; the two squares beside it choose those colours.

Words. Words writes each word's EVA spelling in its box once the zoom makes it big enough to read, from about 2 px/mm. Words on the face underneath are written the right way round, for reading, at their mirrored places. On rings and turned lines a word is written along the writing, its bottom towards the wheel's centre, at one size for the page, sized by its length along that direction rather than its box's width; Word direction: Level stands those words upright at the same size, where they can run past their boxes.

Margins. Each page's text edges, in its colour. A dot on the left is the left end of a full paragraph row that is not its paragraph's first, as the Page stream's margin points; a dot on the right is the right end of a full paragraph row that is not its paragraph's last, which stops short. A row is full when every one of its words is located, in at least three boxes. A line joins only consecutive rows, so a gap stays a gap. The trace follows the writing, so where a line goes round a drawing or stops short, so does the trace. Behind, mirrored, the trace is dashed, and a page's left edge lies on the right.

Text angle. A solid line at each page's median line angle as its words are drawn, with the level dotted through the same point and as long: in Flat coordinates the angle after flattening, which takes the page's tilt out (a tenth of a degree at the median), and in Recorded the angle as written (about a degree). The line goes through the centroid of the page's paragraph text, as wide as the text, or, under View, through the middle of the page, or 100 mm long on every page, so the angles can be compared side by side. Exaggerate draws every line that many times as steep (×2 to ×100, never past 85°), since most pages lie within a degree or two of one another, and after flattening within a few tenths; the values still give the true angle, and a note in the table's top right corner says how much the lines are drawn up. Its value stands just past the line's right end, positive where the lines fall to the right, and Values, under View, turns the values off; hovering a word names the page and gives both angles. Behind, the line is mirrored with its page and dashed, its value past the left end, still the angle as written. Both the angle and the margins are measured against the photograph's frame, not the edge of the parchment, and pages with no horizontal paragraph lines have none.

Labels. Sheet names, under View, writes each sheet's name and the face it shows on the sheet, above it, or not at all; Page names writes each page's name at its leaf's corner, above its leaf, or not at all. Above, each name is a small tag in the space over its sheet or leaf, clear of the pages: the tags of sheets lying on one another go side by side, then in rows, and below the sheet where there is no room above, so none covers another or a page. A click on a tag selects its sheet, and dragging it moves the sheet. Copy numbers off leaves the (2) of a second copy out of the names on the table; the Pieces list keeps it.

Lines. Each horizontal paragraph line of a page as plotted, through its words' baseline points, in the page's colour, drawn as the words are (flat or recorded) and never exaggerated. The lines the text angle is the median of are solid, the others dotted. The opacity sliders set the margins, the angles and the lines apart.

The address. The pieces, their places, faces, pages, colours and opacities, and the View choices stay in the address, so a link reopens them. Positions are display choices: nothing here is a statistical test.
