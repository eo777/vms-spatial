VMS-SPATIAL

About this viewer

An atlas of the Voynich manuscript's recorded word rectangles, page by page, with optional per-word measures painted on them, and the alignment review that decides which words have a usable position.

What is shown

Every rectangle is an original word box of the Voynichese Project (2014, Apache-2.0), on the coordinate canvas of its page. The text is the study transcription of the ZL transliteration: 34,977 paragraph-text tokens in 4,130 lines of 207 pages, read in the Path 1 order of 50 bifolia. Colors come from one of the measures the toolkit computes: the distinct share of a reading-order window (MATTR), whether a word recurs in its window, the impact of one occurrence on all its windows, the step change when it arrives, the edit distance to earlier words in view, and the diversity of the glyph components of the words in view (effective pair types, H1, H2, JS distance). The group Lines, baselines and warp reads how each line lies on the page: its chord and fit, how far every word stands from them, the shape of the line, its breaks, the page warp, and the ink baselines, x-heights and intrusions measured on the Beinecke scans. Clicking a word shows its window, or its cone and weighted sources with their shares, and its line's numbers.

The Coordinates control draws the boxes as recorded, on the photograph's warp, or flat, with each page's warp removed. The measures stay flat either way. The page image is the photograph, so showing it returns the boxes to recorded coordinates.

Orders and texts

Rows are bifolia (the pages of a sheet kept together) by their first folio or in Path 1 order, or single pages in the page sequence. The Text control switches between the manuscript and any control text prepared with the toolkit (generator streams, Markov chains, natural prose laid into the same lines), each as its own bundle with its own geometry.

Pages without recorded rectangles

The Rosettes foldout (fRos) and f116v have no Voynichese rectangles, and twenty circular pages lie outside the study's paragraph text. The atlas shows them from the toolkit's extension: recorded boxes from the alignment review where the Voynichese Project has them, and for the Rosettes boxes the toolkit detected on the Beinecke scan and linked to the transcription by glyph counts and the transcriber's placement notes, each with a confidence. Those boxes are marked detected; they are positions found on the image, not recorded coordinates (see docs/ROSETTES.md in the repository).

Comparing pages

The Compare page lays the text block of one page over another's after a single translation (the first box's baseline anchor, or the page's opening anchor, becomes the origin) and reports how much occupied area the two blocks share (Dice), how alike the wording is where the boxes coincide (direct EVA) or lie near each other (nearby EVA), and the Jensen-Shannon distance of their glyph-pair histograms. The same page shows two pages side by side, several pages in one composite, the word-segment correspondences, the ranked first-line matches of every page and bifolium face, the pre-correction drift guide, an inline reader for any pair of lines, and the circular text of a page with its fitted wheels moved to one centre. The scores describe resemblance after a translation; they do not test copying, and the centred wheels are a display transform (docs/COMPARE.md, docs/WHEELS.md). Every view is a link, and the numbers are those of vmsspatial.compare.

The page stream

The Page stream runs whole pages in a chosen order: a chain of bifolia with four pages to a row, one continuous column, or laid over each other. Each page can take back its recorded tilt, and each bifolium can overlap the last by a chosen amount, to see whether margins and angles carry from sheet to sheet. Joins and overlaps are display choices, placed by the extent of the text and not by the edge of the paper. The default tilt keeps the recorded margin; a rigid turn is offered but leans each drawn margin by the page's own turn (docs/STREAM.md).

Word search lights up the words that match a pattern on every bifolium at once. Each bifolium is a small tile of its pages, up to six patterns run in their own colours, and the sheets go in any order. Patterns can test spellings, the words before and after, and where a word stands on its line, paragraph and page. A page opens to show its matches on its image.

The alignment review

The alignment review page shows, for every page, how each ZL word is associated with the recorded rectangles, and which assignments are shared or disputed. Only reviewed word-level assignments enter spatial calculations; shared boxes keep their complete text but no invented word position.

Build your own

The viewer reads data/index.json, data/layout.json.gz, the alignment review that index.json names, and one words.json.gz bundle per text, all written by vmsspatial viewer-data. A bundle is a plain JSON structure (words, lines, geometry, components) that any other viewer can read, and a study can add its own per-word measures, page overlays and line notes to one (vmsspatial.viewer_data.study_data) and open this atlas on its data folder with #data=<url>, copying nothing; the page renderer (atlas.js) and the measures (../js/) are small standalone scripts.
