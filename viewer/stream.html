    How to read this

    Arrangements. Bifolium chain stacks every bifolium of the sequence, one row a sheet, four pages to a row. Continuous column runs the complete pages top to bottom. Superimposed overlay lays the pages over each other in one fixed frame, and you step through them. No arrangement mirrors a page: all the text stays upright. Ar is A recto (outer), Av A verso (inner), Br B recto (inner), Bv B verso (outer). These are positions on the sheet, which are not always facing pages in the bound book.

    Joins. A continuous chain crops the empty space outside each bifolium's combined text and starts each row where the last one ends. The four pages of a row stay locked together, so a shorter page keeps the blank space beside a longer one, and only whole rows move. Drag a ↕ label to overlap a bifolium onto the one before, from 0 to 95% of that one's text height. This is an arrangement for looking at, not a measurement of how the sheets lay: it uses the text's extent, not the edge of the paper.

    Covered area. Where a row overlaps the one before, the older text below the fold can stay as faint text, box outlines or the margin trace alone, disappear, or stay at full strength. This changes only what is drawn. The fold, the angles and the geometry stay as they are.

    Tilt. Flat coordinates take out each page's warp, and with it the page's overall tilt. Flattening moves words up and down only, so Recorded, true margins puts the tilt back the same way: each line returns to its recorded angle, every x stays where it was recorded, and only the local warp stays out. Rigid turn rotates the page by the same angle instead. A rotation moves x as well, which flattening never did, so each drawn margin leans by the page's own turn on top of its true slope: compare margins only under the first setting, or in recorded coordinates. The side labels give each page's recorded tilt: positive falls to the right. The angle is the median over lines with at least 4 located boxes spanning at least 3 median word widths, so a page with no such line shows none and keeps its flat tilt. This restores an orientation for inspection, not a physical page edge.

    Align. Source x keeps each page's horizontal position from its source image. Opening word puts the first word of each page at x = 0. Median body margin puts the median left edge of the page's body rows at x = 0. Each is one horizontal translation of the page. The height always comes from the opening baseline, because the source images share no vertical origin. Body height scales each page, in both directions alike, to a common median word height.

    Margin points. A dot is the left end of a complete paragraph row that is not a paragraph's first line, and a line joins only consecutive rows, so a gap stays a gap. Nothing joins one page's margin to the next page's. In the overlay the lines are solid for Ar, dashed for Av, dotted for Br and dash-dot for Bv.

    Orders. By bifolium is the manuscript's own. Path 1 is a proposed glyph-statistical ordering, not an established chronology. Reorder bifolia changes only what you see, and its colours stay with their sheets. Candidate correspondence, under Page positions, walks Ar → Bv → Br → Ar and Av → Av between neighbouring bifolia: a hypothesis to inspect, not an established reading order.

    Foldouts and gaps. Foldout panels keep their own names and stack within their slot. Missing positions and pages without an opening box are named, and nothing stands in for them.
