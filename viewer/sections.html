How to read this

Paragraph polygons. The outer envelope of a paragraph's complete horizontal lines, joined halfway across the whitespace between them. It holds the located text and the gaps inside it, so it is not an ink contour. A missing or rotated line, or an unusually large step, splits the polygon. Click a colour under Page colours, or an outline, to pick it out: with Colour by Bifolium a colour is a whole bifolium. Escape returns.

Edit pages. Check a page or bifolium to add it to the group, uncheck it to remove it, or use the × beside a colour. Search covers the whole manuscript. The heatmap and every layer follow the edited group, colours stay as they were, and the changes are saved in the link.

Heatmap. Each cell shows the share of the group's selected pages with at least one horizontal word box in it. A page votes once however many boxes it has there. A page that could not be aligned counts in the total but adds no boxes. The bands are the same for every group, so groups can be compared, but a box is not ink, and larger cells bridge small gaps.

Align. Page positions keeps each page's layout on one anchor. All paragraph starts moves every paragraph to its own anchor, so later paragraphs line up with first ones. The default anchor takes its height from the opening word and its left position from the leftmost word of the paragraph text, so margins line up even on a page like f84v, whose first paragraph sits at the top right. Opening word uses the first word for both, and the corner anchors use a corner of the complete text, which needs a complete first or last line. Everything meets at the anchor by construction: that convergence is not evidence of a shared template.

Moving the view. Drag to move the pages, at any zoom and past the edge of the frame; the wheel zooms. All panels move together, so they stay comparable. Fit returns.

Marks. Breaks are the transcription's annotated intrusions, drawn between two located words on a horizontal line. A triangle marks a paragraph's start and a square its end, on complete lines only. Marks are not normalised by group size, so a larger group looks denser. Rings and rays are not drawn here: see Wheels in Compare.
