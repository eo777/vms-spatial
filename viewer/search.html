Pattern syntax
WriteMeaningExample
  • A pattern matches the whole spelling of a word: aiin finds only the word aiin, and *aiin* finds every word containing it. Click an example to try it.
  • Operator words are in capitals, because lowercase or is a Voynich word.
  • NOT or ! before a term negates all of it, spelling included: ![pgi]p* is every word except the page-initial p-words.
  • ! inside the brackets negates the place only: [!pgi]p* is the p-words off a page's first line, the same as p* AND NOT [pgi]*.
  • Qualifiers stack: [pi][li]p* opens a paragraph, and [pi][!pgi]p* is [lpi]p*.
  • With an offset, write the offset first: -1:[li]*.
  • Neighbours never cross a line end or an unreadable token, so a word with none there does not match -1:*.

How to read this

Tiles. Each tile is one bifolium and each small panel one page. Grey marks are word boxes and coloured marks are matches. A dashed panel is a position with no page in this selection. Foldout panels stay together in their position, so those sheets are wider.

Coordinates and scale. Under Settings. Recorded coordinates put the words where they sit on the photograph; Flat takes out each page's shared curve and tilt (the dewarped state), moving words up and down only. Page photograph draws each page's photograph frame at the tile height. Parchment draws each page on its leaf, in millimetres from the fold and the leaf's top edge, measured on the Beinecke scans or estimated as on the Page stream, so in Physical faces the two pages of a face meet at the fold. An open page always stays in recorded coordinates, because only those sit on the image.

Counts. A count is of words in the transliteration. Shares are over the words where the pattern can match: [pi]p* is out of the words on paragraph-initial lines, and spellings and neighbours never narrow that base. No share is given over fewer than 20 words. A word matching two patterns counts under both and is drawn split between their colours.

What is not drawn. A few words have no box, so a count can run past the marks on a tile: the count line says how many. Where one box holds several words, it lights up when any of them matches, and the count is still of words. Tokens with an unreadable glyph never match, and no neighbour term reaches across one, or across a line end.

Order. Drag a tile by its heading, or use Alt + ← → on its grip. All of a sheet's pages move together. Path 1 is a proposed glyph-statistical ordering, not an established chronology. A custom order stays in the address and carries over to the Page stream, and Saved order reads the boundary sets saved there in this browser.

A page. Click a page to open it. Page image puts the matches on the Voynichese display image, in that image's own word rectangles. The wheel zooms, dragging pans and a double click returns to the whole page. Tiles and pages draw positions and search spellings: nothing here is a statistical test.
